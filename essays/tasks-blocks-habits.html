Tasks, Blocks and Habits

November 2026. 454 words.

Tasks are units of commitment. Blocks are time-bound units of effort. Habits are automated decisions.

Three units, and each one answers a different question.

UnitWhat it isCounted byFails as
TaskA commitment: what counts as done, and by whenDone or not doneA broken promise
BlockEffort with a fixed end: 25 minutes, aimed at a taskBlocks a day, against a targetDrift: the time went somewhere else
HabitA decision made once, then not againTimes a weekThe same decision, made again every day

Tasks are containers

A task is a promise, to someone or to the person I’ll be tomorrow (manage commitments, not tasks). It says what will exist and by when. It doesn’t say how long.

So a task is a container. It holds the finish line, the date, and the blocks it takes. David Cain’s line: “if it can be done, it can be done in some number of Blocks.” Filling the container is the work. The finish line says when it’s full.

Blocks are how I pay

A block is effort with an end fixed in advance. It doesn’t promise an output, only 25 minutes toward one. That’s why it can be started when the task can’t be finished today.

Time is the resource, and the block is its unit. A count of blocks counts starts, and starting is the hard part.

Habits are decisions I stop making

A habit doesn’t go through choosing at all. The cue comes, the action follows. Wendy Wood found that about 43 percent of everyday actions run this way (the levers on how you respond).

That’s why the base is habits: water, medication, exercise. On a bad day there’s nothing to decide. And every rep makes the next one easier, so the cost keeps falling.

How they fit

  • Tasks are what I promise. Blocks are how I pay. Habits keep me able to pay.
  • The task decides when it’s done. The block decides when to stop. “Work on the essay until it’s done” is a task pretending to be a block. Twenty-five minutes with no task is a block with nothing to pay for.
  • A decision I make every day about the same thing is a habit waiting to be made (when this, do that).
  • In the layers of effectiveness, tasks sit in plans, blocks in focused work, and habits in energy, where they can automate any layer above.

The test

For anything on my list, ask which one it is. A promise gets a finish line and a date. Effort gets a number of blocks. A repeated decision gets turned into a habit, so I stop deciding.
