The Other: Trust the System, Not the Brain

November 2026. 441 words.

The “other”: why a system, a person or something external is needed for an ADHD brain.

Trust the system, not the brain.

The missing inner other

Most people carry an other inside. It’s the voice that says “not now,” “first this,” “you’ll regret it later.” It holds the plan in mind, feels time passing, stops the impulse, and gets going without a reward in sight.

Barkley describes ADHD as a problem with exactly that: self-regulation. As he puts it, it isn’t a problem of knowing what to do, but of doing what you know. The inner other is weak. So it has to come from outside.

Three kinds of other

  • A system. A list, a board, a sticky note, a timer, an alarm, an automation. It holds the plan so my head doesn’t have to.
  • A person. Someone to work beside, report to, or review with: a friend, a coach, a therapist, a reviewer. A person adds stakes no list can.
  • Something external. A place, a deadline, money on the line, an environment that makes the right thing easy and the wrong thing hard.

Each one speaks the language ADHD hears: now, seen, felt, concrete.

Trust the system, not the brain

In the moment, the brain makes confident forecasts. “I’ll remember.” “I’ll do it later.” “This will take five minutes.” Most of them are wrong.

The system was set up by a calmer brain, the night before, with the whole picture in view. So when the two disagree, the system wins. That’s Ulysses tied to the mast: he knew what his ears would want later, and he set up the other before it started.

David Allen’s line fits: your mind is for having ideas, not holding them.

It isn’t a weakness

Glasses are an other for the eyes. Pilots and surgeons run checklists not because they forget more than others, but because the cost of one miss is too high. ADHD just needs the other sooner, and for more of the day.

What makes the other work

  • It has to reach me. A system I have to remember to open is one more thing to forget. Automation, alarms and things in plain sight come to me.
  • It has to be trusted. If it misses things, the brain goes back to holding them. So everything goes in, and it gets reviewed every week.
  • One place. Two systems are no system. The brain starts keeping track of which one is right.
  • Don’t make one person the whole system. It turns a partner into a parent. Let the system carry the routine, and let people bring the stakes and the review.
