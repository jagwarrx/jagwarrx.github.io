The Chupacabra

September 2020. 387 words.

This is a (very) short story I wrote back in high school and published in my college magazine. I revisited it recently. Fun read!

Click the field to release a sheep.

With a smile he sat down to enjoy his coffee. Soft Salsa played overhead, and rain thumped against the window beside him. He'd ordered an Alto Grande — the best money could buy. Staring contentedly at the rich volcanic soil of the rural Puerto Rican midlands, he savored the exotic flavors of his beverage.

He turned to watch the news on the small TV. The plump newsreader seemed visibly excited. He could not understand her fluent Spanish, but he soon found out what the fuss was about. Apparently, rumors were rife about a monster. Termed ‘Chupacabra’ by the media, it was responsible for the disappearance of sheep and chicken in villages across the Yabucoa state. People who saw it described it as being out of this world.

Try 'out of their heads' thought the man, smirking to himself. There was no limit to how much people could deceive themselves. Extra-terrestrials travelling a few million light years... to gobble up sheep... in middle of nowhere? For this excellent coffee maybe, but certainly not for adulterated mutton. After their coffee break they might even take the time to fix up this desolate place. Bring a bit of safety to these vulnerable people, and all.

He knew the truth. The sheep were being rustled by a band of outlaws with a strange fashion sense. A rough sketch of the Chupacabra popped up on the TV screen - something that looked like a hybrid between a monkey and a kangaroo. 'Oh these people and their wild imagination!' he laughed inwardly as he faced the cashier across the counter with a suppressed snicker:

“Don’t worry about that monster. It's be taken care of,” he winked at her.

He had handled the cattle rustlers alright. They were bundled up, awaiting transport.

He paid for his coffee and walked outside. The air was crisp and refreshing, like rain at the end of a muggy day. He started on foot and walked past the Arecibo radio telescope. A signboard identified it as the world’s largest single-dish radio telescope. It was used extensively by SETI – Search for Extraterrestrial Life Institute.

‘The irony!’ he thought as he stepped into his spaceship, smiling.
