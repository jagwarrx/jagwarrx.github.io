The Automated Morning Brief

November 2026. 557 words.

I don’t have this yet. This is the case for building it.

The idea is one short message that arrives every morning without my asking. It says what to do today: the three items, the first step of the first one, and the fixed times of the day. Nothing else.

How it would help

Each part answers one of the constraints I have to design around.

It comes to me. Out of sight is out of mind, and a list I have to remember to open is out of sight. A brief that arrives by itself doesn’t depend on my remembering anything.

It makes today a separate thing. I have no feeling for the future, so a full list feels like everything is due now. The brief shows only today: three items, and the times that are fixed.

It puts time where I can see it. I don’t feel time passing. An appointment at two is easy to lose until it’s two. The brief says it first thing.

It hands me the first step. Starting is the hardest part. If the first step of the first item is already written, the day begins with an action and not a decision.

It keeps me out of the big list. The morning is when I’m most likely to open everything and start sorting. With the brief I don’t open it at all. I want to work, not organize.

Where it fits

It adds nothing to the two lists. It reads the list with everything and the calendar, and it delivers the short list. I copy the three items onto the sticky note and start.

It’s the organizing, done out of sight. The sorting happens before I’m up, and what reaches me is three lines.

What it must not do

Choose for me. The three items are picked the night before, by me. The brief delivers them. A machine choosing my day is an order, and orders are easy to ignore.

Grow. No scores, no charts, no summary of yesterday. Once it’s long, it’s another list to scan.

Wait to be opened. If I have to go and get it, it has the same problem as the app it replaced.

Building it in practice

It needs four components, and I have most of them already.

A capture point. A daily note or inbox in the notes tool I already use.

A store the AI can read and write. My notes app and calendar, connected to an assistant.

Scheduled agents. A nightly inbox processor, a morning brief, and a weekly review prep.

A short instruction file. My projects, my priorities, and my filing conventions, so the AI’s guesses match how I think.

The brief is the part I see. The nightly processor moves what I captured into the list with everything, and the weekly prep gets the weekend sitting ready. Neither needs me to watch.

The cheap test

Automating it is the last step, not the first. The quicker way is to ask for the brief by hand every morning for a week. If I read it and the day goes better, it’s worth automating. If I skip it by Wednesday, automation would only deliver something I ignore, on time.

It can also fail the way everything else does. If it arrives somewhere I don’t look, it doesn’t exist.
