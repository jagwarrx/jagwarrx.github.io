Religion as a Collective of the Higher Self

November 2026. 478 words.

In Free Up the Higher Mind I wrote that God and religion are an expression of the higher mind, and that it’s there in all of us. Take one more step and religion is a collective of the higher self: the higher mind of many people, organized. Shared rituals, texts, calendars and a community, all built to help each person act from the higher self more often. It’s the same move as a personal habit, made at the scale of a people.

But anything collective can be captured. Religion gets captured in two ways, and both swap the higher self for the lower one while keeping its clothes.

Weaponized religion

The first capture is by power. The group identity is kept and the inner practice is dropped. Then religion becomes us against them, a reason for war, a tool to win votes or hold a throne. That’s the side I looked at in Balance Is the Ground, Not the Goal: devout societies aren’t reliably peaceful, because religion as group identity sharpens us-versus-them, while religion as practice makes people gentler. The Nation as Religion shows the same machinery running without a god.

The tell is what gets asked of you. The higher self asks you to change yourself. A weaponized religion asks you to change, or remove, someone else.

Commercial spirituality

The second capture is by money. The practice is kept and the point is dropped. Meditation sold as a productivity tool, retreats as a luxury good, wisdom cut into pieces that fit a feed. The Market for the Need traces how religion came to be sold in pieces. Ronald Purser calls the mindfulness version McMindfulness: the technique without the ethics. Long before, Chögyam Trungpa named the inner version spiritual materialism: the ego taking up spiritual practice to make itself bigger.

The tell here is direction. Real practice makes the self lighter. Commercial spirituality adds to it: more to buy, more to be.

Both captures are the same thing underneath. A lower game, power or money, playing inside a higher one (Power Is a Means).

Goodwill with discernment

Since not everyone acts from their higher self, goodwill isn’t the same as naivety. Yours needs discernment too. The Gospel puts it as being “wise as serpents, and harmless as doves.” (The Serpent and the Dove) It’s a barbell, as in Arrange Things So You Don’t Need to Know: goodwill as the default, firm boundaries as protection.

Ramana Maharshi would add something else. People often asked him how to fix the world, and he usually turned the question back: find out who it is that wants to fix it. In his view, living from the Self is the most useful thing anyone can do for the world, because peace spreads through people who have it. That’s the one part of this that’s in your control. The rest you let play out.
