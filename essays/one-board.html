See Everything Together in One Place

November 2026. 475 words.

To make a decision, I need to see everything together in one place. One master board, with every live thing on it, all visible at once.

It’s an ADHD trait, and it’s about uncertainty. A decision made with part of the picture is a decision made in doubt, and doubt is what stops me deciding.

The trait

If I don’t see it, it doesn’t exist. It’s the first constraint I design around. A project in another app, a task two clicks deep, a date in a calendar I didn’t open: for me, at the moment of choosing, they don’t exist.

I can’t hold the pieces in my head. Comparing three things in three places means carrying two of them in working memory while I look at the third. That’s the part ADHD is short of. So the comparison doesn’t happen, or it happens badly.

The doubt stops the decision. When things are spread out, every choice comes with a second question: is there something I’m missing? I don’t know, so the choice feels unsafe. I check again, or I put it off, or I pick whatever is in front of me. It looks like indecision. It’s uncertainty.

What one board does

It closes the set. If it’s not on the board, it isn’t live. That turns “what am I missing?” from an open question into a closed one, and choosing becomes comparing what’s in front of me.

It shows conflicts. Two things due the same week. Five things started at once. Everything waiting on the same person. Spread out, those stay hidden until they hurt. Together, I can see them.

It quiets the background. We need to know what comes next. A board doesn’t tell me the future, but it tells me everything I’ve said yes to. That’s most of the uncertainty I can do anything about.

How I keep it

One board. Not one per area or one per app. One.

Everything on it is current. A dead card is noise, and noise brings the doubt back. Done or dropped comes off.

One card per thing, in short words. The board is read at a glance, so it’s written in a visual language: a few signs and a few words per card.

No scrolling, no opening. If I have to click to see it, it’s out of sight again.

Decide on the board, work from the list

This looks like it fights looking at one item at a time. It doesn’t. They’re for different moments. Deciding wants to see everything. Doing wants to see almost nothing.

So I look at the whole board at set times: the night before, to pick tomorrow’s three, and once a week, to see the whole shape. Then I work from the short list. The board makes the decision safe. The list makes the work possible.
