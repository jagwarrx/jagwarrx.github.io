Life, Quarter, Week, Day

November 2026. 344 words.

Two questions after Happiness Can Be Designed, Partly. Are connection, presence and progress values or principles? And what are the scales of a life: the scale of life, of a quarter, of a day? Does each one need a strategy?

Values or principles

In the terms of Principles Are Guiding Policy for a Life, the three are mostly descriptive principles: findings about how happiness works, like “trust builds slowly.” The value underneath them is wellbeing.

A description doesn’t tell you what to do. To act on them, rewrite each as an “A over B” principle:

  • Connection: people over screens.
  • Presence: here over elsewhere.
  • Progress: progress over perfection.

The scales

Life, quarter and day are close to what Cal Newport calls multi-scale planning: quarterly, weekly and daily plans, anchored by a separate values document as a timeless guide. The week is the scale I left out, and it’s what connects the quarter to the day.

Each scale needs a different kind of guidance. Only one needs a strategy in Rumelt’s sense.

  • Life: principles. A lifetime is too long and changes too much for one strategy. This is the stake: values and principles, reviewed about once a year.
  • Quarter: strategy. Diagnose this season’s main challenge, choose a guiding policy, pick two or three coherent actions. A quarter is long enough to get something done, and short enough to give you four chances a year to fix what’s broken.
  • Week: rhythm. The repeating policies: the standing meal with friends, the pattern of hard effort and real rest. Connection lives at this scale.
  • Day: execution and presence. Plan the hours, then be in them. This is where happiness is felt and small wins happen.

Two rules

Each scale serves the one above it. The days carry out the quarter, and the quarters express the principles.

And information flows back up. What the days teach you should change the quarter, and what the quarters teach you can revise the principles. That’s the correct-as-you-go loop from Balance Is the Ground, Not the Goal, run at four speeds.
