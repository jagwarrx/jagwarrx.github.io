Free Up the Higher Mind

November 2026. 338 words.

Two things in us affect each other, to different extents. Humans have different levels of divinity and badness in them. Communities are shaped by this, and so are whole societies. And the same person, at different times, has different levels.

The first move is to observe. Learn more about how you are affected, by what and when, and you adapt faster.

The aim isn’t to remove one side. What you need is a healthy balance, the kind I described in Balance Is the Ground, Not the Goal: not the top, but what lets the higher things grow. The old phrase for it is to act in the world without being of this world.

Here’s how I picture it. Software runs on hardware. The hardware is focused on survival: the monkey brain, built for threats, food and the next thing, the same machinery as in The Need Is Built In. The part above it is focused on greater things. God, religion and the rest are an expression of that higher mind, and it’s there in all of us.

The higher mind runs on the same hardware, so every small decision takes from the same budget. Habits, rituals, rules (if this, then that) and “following a system” are ways of automating yourself. They hand the routine to the hardware and free up mental hardware for the higher mind. The mathematician Alfred North Whitehead said the same about civilization: it advances by extending the number of important operations we can perform without thinking about them. It’s why, for attention, I want automation, not tools, and why a ritual works best when it’s simple enough to need nothing.

I think cleanliness is a result of the higher mind too. Besides making social life easier, it reduces complexity and conserves energy. A clean space is fewer things for the hardware to track.

So: do more from the higher mind. Watch how the two sides move you, keep them in balance, and automate the rest so there’s room for it.

Notes

  1. “In the world but not of it” is a paraphrase of John 17:14–16. The Bhagavad Gita has the same idea at 5.10: one who acts without attachment is untouched, as a lotus leaf by water.
  2. Whitehead, An Introduction to Mathematics, 1911, chapter 5.
