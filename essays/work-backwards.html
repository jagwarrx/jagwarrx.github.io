Always Work Backwards in a Plan

November 2026. 467 words.

Always work backwards in a plan. Start at the finish line and ask what has to be true just before it. Then what has to be true before that. Keep going until you reach something you can do today.

And once the plan is walked back, sometimes deep strategy isn’t needed. Just raw firepower to complete things.

Why backwards

Every step is there for a reason. Plan forwards and I start from what I know, what’s easy and what’s interesting, and the plan drifts toward those. Attention goes to what’s new, not to what the end needs. Walk back from the end, and each step exists only because the next one needs it. Nothing extra gets in.

It shows the real size. Forwards, a plan feels about right until the last week. Backwards, I lay the steps against the date, and if the walk lands before today, the plan is already late. Better to find that out now than at the end.

It finds what has to start first. The step that takes longest, or waits on someone else, shows up early in the walk back. That’s the one to start this week, even though it isn’t the first step in order.

It ends at a start. The walk only stops at something I can do today. So the plan never ends in a fog. It ends at a first step.

The idea is old. Stephen Covey made it a habit: begin with the end in mind. Teachers call it backward design: decide what students should be able to do at the end, then build the lessons back from there.

Sometimes it’s just firepower

Working backwards does a second job. It tells me what kind of problem I have.

If I can walk all the way back to today without getting stuck, the path is clear. Nothing important is uncertain. Then the problem isn’t thinking. It’s volume: blocks, hours, reps. Strategy is for when the way through is unclear. When it’s clear, more strategy doesn’t help. What helps is raw firepower: count the blocks it needs, put a number on it, and fire.

Over-thinking a clear task is also a way to avoid it. Planning feels like progress, and for a brain like mine it wins against the real work every time. If the steps are already known, another sitting with the plan is organizing, not work.

If the walk back stalls, if there’s a step I can’t name or can’t size, that’s where the uncertainty is. That one step gets thinking, or a small experiment. Only that step. Everything around it is still firepower.

The short version

Walk back from the end to today. Where the walk goes through cleanly, stop planning and do the volume. Where it stalls, think, and only there.
