Modelling ADHD Attention as a Stack

November 2026. 415 words.

ADHD attention works like a stack. The last thing in is the first thing out. Whatever is on top is top of mind, and everything under it might as well not exist.

How the stack behaves

  • Push. Anything new goes on top: a notification, a thought, a person at the door. It takes the attention at once.
  • Top of mind. Only the top item gets attention. Not the most important one, the newest one.
  • Buried. The important task is still in there, under three newer things. Out of sight, so it doesn’t exist.
  • Leaky. In a real stack, finishing the top item brings back the one below. Mine leaks: pop the interruption and the task under it is gone. Something new gets pushed instead.
  • Overflow. Too many pushes at once and the whole stack drops. That’s overwhelm, or the freeze.

Why it explains the monkey wrench

It explains the list. New things get immediate attention because they’re pushed on top. Important things get deprioritized because importance doesn’t move an item up. Only being new does. And things wait till they seem dangerous because danger is the strongest push there is.

A stack, not a priority queue

What I want is a priority queue: the most important item comes out first, whenever it went in. My head won’t do that. So build the queue outside, on paper or a board, and let the head be a stack. The job is then to control what’s on top.

Working with it

  • Push the important thing first. Decide it the night before, put it on a sticky note, and make it the first thing on the stack in the morning.
  • Catch pushes before they land. A scrap pad takes the new thought, so it goes on the list instead of on top of my head (Improvised Productivity).
  • Leave a breadcrumb before every push. One line on what I was doing. When the interruption pops, there’s something to return to.
  • Re-push after interruptions. Look at the board and put the important thing back on top, on purpose.
  • Keep the stack shallow with a WIP limit. A cap on work in progress, from kanban: nothing new starts until something finishes. One thing at a time in the head, and three items at most on the list of what matters now. The limit is what stops the overflow.
  • Fewer pushes. Notifications off, door closed, phone in another room. Every push I stop is one less thing on top.
