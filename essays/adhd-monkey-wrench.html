The ADHD Monkey Wrench: Diffused Attention

November 2026. 379 words.

The ADHD monkey wrench: diffused attention.

It affects sustained attention. I can focus only when something is novel or high stakes. So:

  • I postpone things till they seem dangerous.
  • Important things get deprioritized.
  • New things get immediate attention.
  • Risky behaviors get normalized.
  • Focus drifts.

In high-stakes situations, like holding a newborn, I feel in the moment, and I feel “myself.”

Interest, not importance

The psychiatrist William Dodson puts it this way: most people’s attention runs on importance. It matters, so they do it. ADHD attention runs on interest: novelty, challenge, urgency, something I care about. Importance alone doesn’t switch it on.

That explains the whole list. The important thing has importance and nothing else, so it waits. The new thing has novelty, so it wins.

Why danger works

Waiting until it’s dangerous isn’t laziness. Danger is urgency, and urgency is one of the few switches that reliably works.

The cost is running on danger. Each last-minute rescue is a rep, and every rep makes the next one easier. Waiting for danger becomes the habit, and the risk starts to feel normal.

The newborn

Holding a newborn, the stakes turn attention on and hold it there. No effort, no drift. That’s the feeling of being “myself.”

It’s also the evidence: the attention isn’t missing. It’s there, at full strength, when the conditions are right. The problem is regulating it, not having it.

Borrow the stakes

The fix isn’t to wait for danger. It’s to flip the switches on purpose, early, in small doses:

  • Urgency: a timer and a block. Many short deadlines, not one far one.
  • Novelty: a new place, a new format, a new angle on the same task.
  • Stakes with people: tell someone, work beside someone, put another person in the date.
  • Interest: find the real question inside the task.
  • Visibility: put the important things where I look, so importance can compete with new.
  • Rules made in advance: important first; new things go on a list, not into the day (when this, do that).
  • Medication: turns the noise down, so the switches need less force.

Where it sits

Attention is a resource, so ADHD is a monkey wrench at the bottom of the stack. Until it’s managed, everything above it pays.
