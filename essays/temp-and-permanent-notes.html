Temp Notes and Permanent Notes

November 2026. 347 words.

I keep two kinds of notes. Temp notes are day to day. Permanent notes are the ones I mean to keep.

KindWhat goes inWhere
TempDay to day: how to start a block, a stray thought, what came upAntinote
PermanentOutputs of projects, decisions I want to record, listsThe permanent notes list
ReminderSomething that has to come back at a timeThe timeline

Temp notes

A temp note empties my head so the block can keep going. It’s written fast and read once, if at all. Antinote does this well: it’s the scratchpad in the minimum viable tool, next to the timer.

Temp notes are allowed to go. Their job is done when the thought is out of my head.

Permanent notes

  • Outputs of projects. What a project leaves behind once it’s done. Like artifacts, they’re resources, managed a level above the minimum (Spruced Up Planka).
  • Decisions I want to record. Priorities every quarter, for example. Written down, a decision doesn’t have to be made again, and the next review has something to check against (projects and objectives).
  • Lists. The things I like can be one note on the permanent notes list. A list of lists, one note each (a list of lists).

Reminders

If I need a reminder, I put it on the timeline. A note can’t remind me of anything: it waits for me to open it. The timeline is where time-bound things go, so a reminder that sits there comes up when its day does.

The test

For anything in a temp note, ask what it is. An output, a decision or something for a list: it moves to the permanent notes. Something that has to come back at a time: it goes on the timeline. Anything else stays temp, and goes.

The split is like the one in Sönke Ahrens’s How to Take Smart Notes, where fleeting notes are thrown away and permanent notes are kept. Mine is cut by what a note is for: getting through today, or keeping.
