If You Don’t See It, It Doesn’t Exist

November 2026. 317 words.

If I don’t see it, it doesn’t exist. For me that isn’t a figure of speech. It’s how my attention works, and it’s the first constraint I design around.

People with ADHD often joke that they have no object permanence. It isn’t literally true; object permanence is a baby’s milestone, from Piaget. But it names the experience well: the thing is still there, and it has stopped counting.

What it covers

Tasks. A task two clicks deep inside a project is the same as a task I never wrote down. If it’s buried, I won’t do it.

Time. A deadline next week doesn’t exist until it’s tomorrow. Then it exists all at once, and that flip is a panic spike.

Tools. A tool I don’t open doesn’t exist. It works while it’s new, and stops the week I forget it.

Decisions. If the options are in three places, I choose from the one in front of me. To decide well, I need to see everything together.

Use it both ways

The trait isn’t only a problem. It’s a lever, and it pulls in two directions.

To keep something alive, put it in view. A sticky note on the desk. One board with every live thing on it. The first step written at the top of the page. Whatever is in view becomes the obvious next thing.

To make something go away, hide it. The phone in another room. The big list touched only at the weekend. The organizing done where I can’t see it. For tasks, out of sight is the problem. For organizing, it’s the point.

The rule

Decide what’s in view, on purpose. I can’t make myself remember the hidden thing. I can choose what isn’t hidden.

And the test for any system is the one from Not Tools, but Automation: does it still work on a day I forget it exists?
