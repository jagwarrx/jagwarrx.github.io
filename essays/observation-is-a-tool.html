Observation Is a Tool

November 2026. 187 words.

You shape your tools, then your tools shape you. It’s usually said about hammers, books and screens. It holds for the tools inside you too.

Observation is one of them. In Free Up the Higher Mind the first move was to observe: learn how you are affected, and you adapt faster. Done often, observation stops being something you do once. It becomes a tool, a faculty of attention. And like any tool, it shapes the one who uses it. What you get good at noticing, you start to live by.

The thing worth training it on is the higher mind. Everyone has access to it. They just need to train their observation on it. There’s nothing to buy or join: it’s the same faculty you use to notice anything else, pointed somewhere else.

Meditation, taken apart, is this training. Shinzen Young’s small words for experience, in Bottom-Up Programming for Enlightenment, are a way of sharpening this one tool.

So the loop runs both ways. You train your observation on the higher mind, and the trained observation makes the higher mind easier to find the next time.

Notes
