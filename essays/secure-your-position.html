Secure Your Position First

November 2026. 318 words.

The first move in The Art of Defense, and the one that does the most work. Before learning any tactic, make yourself hard to pressure.

Beyond the possibility of defeat

Sun Tzu: “The good fighters of old first put themselves beyond the possibility of defeat, and then waited for an opportunity of defeating the enemy.” He points out that protecting yourself from defeat is in your own hands, while the chance to win is handed to you by the opponent. So put your effort into the half you control.

Clausewitz argued that defense is the stronger form of war, because holding ground is easier than taking it. For him defense wasn’t passive. It is “a shield made up of well-directed blows.”

In a life

Your savings, skills, relationships and options form the safe end of your barbell (Arrange Things So You Don’t Need to Know). Manipulation works mostly through need, and it’s hard to pressure someone who can walk away.

Negotiators have a name for this. In Getting to Yes, Fisher and Ury call it your BATNA, the best alternative to a negotiated agreement: what you’ll do if this deal fails. The better your alternative, the more power you have at the table, whatever the other side holds.

  • Savings buy time, so no deadline is really theirs.
  • Skills go with you. Mastery is a position no one can take (Understand Power as Defense).
  • Relationships mean you never decide alone.
  • Options mean you can say no.

Built before the moment

A position can’t be built under pressure. It’s the earliest lever there is, the situation itself (The Levers on How You Respond), and it gets built in calm weeks, long before anyone tests it. It’s also the reason to want some power, and a definition of enough (Power Is a Means): enough that no one can push you around.
