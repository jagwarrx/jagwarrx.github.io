Habits That Sustain

November 2026. 469 words.

Some habits hold the rest up. At the base: exercise, water, medication. On the next level: hygiene, nutrition, sleep, planning. Above them sits everything else: the work, the projects, the people.

I call this the pyramid of habits.

Why a pyramid

Each level holds up the one above it. When the base slips, everything above it wobbles, and no amount of planning fixes a day with no water and no medication. So the order matters: build from the bottom, and when things fall apart, repair from the bottom.

The shape is borrowed from Maslow’s hierarchy of needs, though Maslow never drew it as a pyramid himself. Others did, later. The idea is the same: some things come first, because the rest stands on them.

The base: exercise, water, medication

These three set the state of the body that everything else runs on. State is one of the earliest levers: the same day gets a different response from a body that has moved, drunk water and taken its medication.

Medication turns the noise down. It doesn’t pick itself up from the pharmacy, and it does nothing on the days I forget it.

Exercise helps attention and mood the same day. It lowers anxiety within minutes, and it’s one of the few things that helps both ADHD and anxiety.

Water is the cheapest of the three. Even mild dehydration is linked to worse mood and attention in lab studies.

They make the base because they’re small, daily and easy to check. Each takes minutes. Each is done or not done. A day with these three is a day that can hold weight.

The next level: hygiene, nutrition, sleep, planning

These take more. Sleep is a whole evening protected. Nutrition is shopping and cooking. Hygiene is the routines that keep me and my space in working order. Planning is the night before, deciding tomorrow while I’m calm.

They lean on the base. Exercise helps sleep. A clear head makes the planning possible. Try to build this level on a missing base, and it keeps falling down.

How I use it

On a bad day, the base is the whole goal. If exercise, water and medication happened, the day counted. That’s the minimum, and it’s enough.

When the week falls apart, go back to the base. Not to the plan, not to the system. That’s the response decided in advance: when things collapse, do the three, then build up again.

Keep the base in view and counted. If I don’t see it, it doesn’t exist, so the base goes where I look. And it gets numbers: water at set times, not “drink more.”

Keep the base small. Three habits. Adding a fourth makes the minimum less of a minimum. New habits go on the levels above.

Notes

  1. On the pyramid: Bridgman, Cummings and Ballard, “Who built Maslow’s pyramid?”, Academy of Management Learning & Education, 2019.
  2. On water: Ganio et al., British Journal of Nutrition, 2011; Armstrong et al., Journal of Nutrition, 2012. Both found that mild dehydration, about 1.5 percent of body mass, affected mood, and in men some measures of attention and memory.
