The Language ADHD Speaks

November 2026. 398 words.

ADHD processes a certain kind of language. It hears now, seen, felt, concrete and short. It barely hears importance, the future, abstractions or “should.”

Every system has its own language. This is the language of mine.

What it hears, and what it doesn’t

HearsTunes out
Now: this block, timer runningLater: by Friday, this quarter
Seen: a sticky note, a board, a checkboxStored: in an app, in my head
Concrete: open the doc, write three linesAbstract: work on the essay
Felt: interest, novelty, stakes, a person waitingImportance: it matters
Short: a word, a number, a markLong: paragraphs, rules

Why these words

  • Now. Barkley calls it time blindness: there are only two times, now and not now. “Later” is a word from another language.
  • Seen. Barkley again: the cue has to be at the point of performance, where the action happens. If I don’t see it, it doesn’t exist.
  • Felt. ADHD attention runs on interest, not importance: novelty, challenge, urgency, something I care about.
  • Short and visual. Marks and shapes do organizing work that words do badly, and short words beat sentences.
  • Newest. Only the top of the stack is heard. A message has to land on top, or be put back there.

Managing ADHD is translation

The plan is written in the language of importance: goals, quarters, weeks, “this matters.” The day has to run in the language of now. Most of what goes wrong is a message that never got translated.

“I should really work on the website this week” becomes a sticky note on the laptop: 2 blocks, Language of Systems edits, before lunch, with the timer running and a friend told. Same intention, now in words the brain can hear.

How to translate

  • Give it a time: today, this block, not this week.
  • Put it where I look: on the desk or the screen, not in an app.
  • Name the first physical action: “open the doc,” not “work on it.”
  • Add a stake: a timer, a person, some money (Improvised Productivity).
  • Add a number: two blocks, ten cards, anything but hours.
  • Cut it to one line. If it needs a paragraph, it isn’t translated yet.

The review translates the other way: it takes what the days did and reads it back in the language of importance. Did the things that matter get done?
