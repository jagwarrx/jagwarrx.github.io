The Minimum System for ADHD

November 2026. 333 words.

Two lists. That’s the whole system.

One with everything. One with what’s important now, three items at most.

The list with everything

This one is storage. Anything I might do goes here, so it isn’t lost. It stays in one place. I don’t work from it, and I touch it only on weekends. During the week I pick from it and leave it alone.

The list for now

This one holds what’s important now. Three items at most.

Three, because a list of three can be finished, and because I can hold only a few live things at once. With three there’s nothing to scan and almost nothing to choose.

It’s best picked the night before. Picking means looking at everything, and that’s the overwhelming part, so I get it over with before the day starts.

It’s physical. It goes on a sticky note, never in an app. A sticky note is small and it stays in view, and opening an app means looking at everything again. I can recreate it whenever: if things change, I write a new one. When the three are done, it goes in the bin.

The inbox

New things need somewhere to land. The inbox is part of either list, not a third one. A new item goes on the sticky note or at the end of the big list, whichever is closer.

Why this is the minimum

Take away the list with everything, and things get lost. Out of sight is out of mind, so anything not written down is gone.

Take away the short list, and I’m working from everything. That’s the traditional to-do list, and it doesn’t work: with no feeling for the future, every line on it feels due now.

Add a third thing, like tags, projects, priorities or a dashboard, and it’s upkeep. Upkeep is what I have least of.

So it’s two lists. With fewer, it breaks. With more, I spend the day organizing, and I want to work.
