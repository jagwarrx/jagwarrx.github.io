The goal is not a well-organized life

November 2026. 534 words.

Organizing feels like work. It uses the same screen and the same hours, and I’m just as tired at the end. But when it’s over, nothing is done. The list is tidier and the task is where it was.

I want to work, and not organize all the time.

Why organizing wins

Put a real task next to a chance to organize, and organizing wins on every one of the constraints I have to design around.

It’s easy to start. Starting is the hardest part of real work. Re-sorting a list has no hard first step.

It’s new. A new view, a new app, a new way to group things. My attention goes to what’s new.

It pays right away. A tidy list looks like progress within minutes. The real task pays at the end, if it pays at all.

It never ends. A list can always be sorted one more time, so there’s never a point where I’m forced to stop and do the work.

So this isn’t laziness. Given how my attention runs, organizing is the obvious thing to do. I have to change the conditions, not try harder.

Some of it is needed

I can’t skip organizing. Things have to be written down, put in a list, and given an order, or nothing is in front of me when it’s time to work. That part takes minutes. The trouble is everything past it: sorting again, redesigning the lists, moving to a new system.

The rules I use

One store, and it doesn’t move. Everything lives in one place. I can change how I look at it as often as I like. I don’t change where it lives.

Organize at set times. A few minutes the night before, to pick the next day’s list, and a longer sitting on the weekend. Organizing and doing are separate modes, and I don’t organize in the middle of doing.

Work first. The first block of the day goes to a real task, before anything that touches the system.

Park the ideas. When I think of a better way to organize, I write it down as one line and leave it for the weekend. I don’t build it the same day.

Write the fault first. A change to the system needs a written fault: what went wrong, and when. If I can’t name one, I’m not fixing a problem. I’m looking for something new.

The test

At the end of the day I ask one thing: what exists now that didn’t exist this morning? A page written counts. A call made counts. A list that’s in a better order doesn’t.

A system is there so the work happens. The less time I spend inside it, the better it’s doing its job.

One number

Measure the system by one number: minutes per day you spend on it. If that number rises, the system is failing, however elegant it looks.

The goal

The goal is not a well-organized life. It is a life where the organization happens out of sight and your attention goes to the work.

Out of sight is out of mind. For tasks, that’s the problem. For organizing, it’s the point.
