Projects and Objectives

November 2026. 549 words.

An objective is a concrete goal: get a new job. A project is work with a finish line that moves an objective: finish Hello Interview System Design. Tasks, blocks and habits are what feed them.

Aim at objectives, finish projects

I can’t finish “get a new job” by working. The offer is someone else’s call. I can finish a project: the course is done when the last block is logged. So an objective is something I aim at, and a project is something I complete.

One objective usually takes several projects: the course, the resume, the applications. If every project is done and the objective hasn’t moved, the projects were wrong, not the effort.

What feeds them

Blocks fill tasks. Tasks finish projects. Projects move objectives. Habits keep the energy coming for all of it.

Read upward, that’s also the check. Is this block on a task? Is the task in a project? Does the project move an objective? A no at any step is drift, even when the block was good work.

Where they sit

In Direction, Time, Energy, objectives are direction: the strategy layer made concrete. Projects are the outputs, what will exist in the world. Tasks and blocks are time. Habits are energy. The layer there called “plans and objectives” means this week’s objectives, a smaller use of the word.

In Layers of Operations, planning takes a project and sizes it in blocks. Objectives sit above planning: they decide which projects get planned at all. Each runs on its own clock. Objectives change by the quarter, projects move by the week, blocks by the hour.

A tool for each job

  • Objectives: the visual strategy planner. Its job is to show objectives across time and track progress on each.
  • Projects: Planka. Its job is to let me hold only the work in progress in my attention at one time. Everything else waits on a board, safe to forget. It also keeps what the work leaves behind: notes from blocks, artifacts from projects.
  • Tasks, blocks and habits: the minimum viable tool. Do a block, log it against a project, count it.

Above objectives

Above an objective is the want: why I want the new job at all. In Direction, Time, Energy that’s the ends layer. It’s fuzzy, and it changes. I’m not sure how to put it into words, or if it needs words.

One way to hold it: the want doesn’t need words, it needs a check. Once a quarter, look at each objective and ask if it still pulls. If it doesn’t, change the objective. The want shows up in what I pick.

The model has to be useful, not exact. Two levels I can act on, objectives and projects, and one fuzzy level I only check, is enough.
