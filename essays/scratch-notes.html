Scratch Notes, Permanent Notes and Artifacts

November 2026. 484 words.

What I write comes in three kinds. Scratch notes are day to day. Permanent notes are the ones I mean to keep. Artifacts are what projects make.

KindWhat goes inWhere
Scratch noteDay to day: how to start a block, a stray thought, what came upAntinote
Permanent noteDecisions I want to record, lists, notes a project leaves behindThe permanent notes list
ArtifactWhat a project makes: an essay, an app, a videoAttached to its project
ReminderSomething that has to come back at a timeThe timeline

Scratch notes

A scratch note empties my head so the block can keep going. It’s written fast and read once, if at all. Antinote does this well: it’s the scratchpad in the minimum viable tool, next to the timer.

Scratch notes are allowed to go. Their job is done when the thought is out of my head.

Permanent notes

  • Outputs of projects. What a project leaves behind once it’s done. Like artifacts, they’re resources, managed a level above the minimum (Spruced Up Planka).
  • Decisions I want to record. Priorities every quarter, for example. Written down, a decision doesn’t have to be made again, and the next review has something to check against (projects and objectives).
  • Lists. The things I like can be one note on the permanent notes list. A list of lists, one note each (a list of lists).

Artifacts

Sometimes the output of a project isn’t a note. It’s an artifact: the essay, the app, the video. In Direction, Time, Energy that’s the outputs layer: what will exist in the world.

  • It’s the finish line. A project is done when its artifact exists. Naming the artifact up front makes the finish line concrete.
  • It stays with its project. Each artifact is attached to the project that made it, so I find it where I’d look.
  • It’s a resource. Like permanent notes, artifacts are managed a level above the minimum, on a longer timeframe (Spruced Up Planka). The minimum only counts the blocks that made them.

Reminders

If I need a reminder, I put it on the timeline. A note can’t remind me of anything: it waits for me to open it. The timeline is where time-bound things go, so a reminder that sits there comes up when its day does.

The test

For anything in a scratch note, ask what it is. A decision, something for a list, or what a project leaves behind: it moves to the permanent notes. Something that has to come back at a time: it goes on the timeline. Anything else stays scratch, and goes.

The split is like the one in Sönke Ahrens’s How to Take Smart Notes, where fleeting notes are thrown away and permanent notes are kept. Mine is cut by what a note is for: getting through today, or keeping.
