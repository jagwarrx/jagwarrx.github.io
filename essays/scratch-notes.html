Scratch Notes, Permanent Notes and Artifacts

November 2026. 436 words.

What I write comes in a few kinds, one for each layer. Scratch notes are for the block. Project notes are for the project. Artifacts are what’s worth keeping once it’s cleaned up.

Scratch notes

The scratchpad is the blocks layer. On paper or in Antinote, next to the timer (minimum viable tool). A scratch note empties my head so the block can keep going. It’s written fast and read once, if at all.

Scratch notes are temp. Their job is done when the thought is out of my head.

Project notes

Project notes hold what’s needed to achieve an objective: the mindmap of what the project takes, the pieces, the open questions. They live in Heptabase or Excalidraw, where I can see the whole thing at once. They last as long as the project.

Artifacts

An artifact is cleaned up project notes that can be reused later. When a project ends, most of its notes have done their job. The parts I’d want again get cleaned up into an artifact, so the next project starts from them instead of from nothing.

Artifacts are resources, managed a level above the minimum, on a longer timeframe (Spruced Up Planka).

Permanent notes

  • Decisions I want to record. Priorities every quarter, for example. Written down, a decision doesn’t have to be made again, and the next review has something to check against.
  • Lists. The things I like can be one note on the permanent notes list (a list of lists).

Reminders

If I need a reminder, I put it on the timeline. A note can’t remind me of anything: it waits for me to open it. On the timeline, a reminder comes up when its day does.

The test

For anything in a scratch note, ask where it goes. Part of a project’s thinking: project notes. A decision or something for a list: permanent notes. Something that has to come back at a time: the timeline. Anything else goes.

Sönke Ahrens’s How to Take Smart Notes has a similar split: fleeting notes are thrown away, project notes stay with one project, and permanent notes are kept for good.
