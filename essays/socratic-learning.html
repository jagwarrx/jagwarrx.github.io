Learning with the Socratic Method

November 2026. 284 words.

The best use of an LLM is learning with the Socratic method. Not asking it for answers. Asking it to teach me by making me think.

An answer feels like learning, and it fades by the next day. A question I had to work out stays. The model can do the one thing a book can’t: find the exact spot where my understanding is thin, and push on it.

How the tutor works

  • Open with a diagnostic question. Find out what I already know before teaching anything.
  • Alternate. A short burst of real teaching, then one question. Then teaching, then a question.
  • One question at a time. Never a list. One question I can actually answer.
  • Aim at the joints. The questions go to where my reasoning connects one step to the next. That’s where shallow spots hide.
  • Repair, then move on. When a shallow spot surfaces, fix it right there, then keep going.

When to use it

  • “Be my tutor for X.”
  • “Teach me X.”
  • “Help me actually understand X.”
  • “Quiz me on X” or “test my understanding.”
  • “Walk me through X, but make me think.”
  • “Drill me on this syllabus for an exam.”

Not for a quick fact or a definition. Those just want the answer.

Why it suits me

  • It keeps me in the loop. Reading an explanation, my attention drifts. A question waiting for me pulls it back.
  • The steps are small. One burst, one question. One thing at a time, again.
  • I find out what I don’t know. Feeling I understand and being able to answer are different things. The question shows which one I have.
  • The answer is mine. When I work it out, it compounds.
