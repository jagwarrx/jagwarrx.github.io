Minimum Viable Tool

November 2026. 632 words.

The smallest kit that runs my days. Most of it is paper. The app part is one small page.

The kit

  • Do a block, then log it against a project.
  • Count the blocks each day on a sticky note, and have a target.
  • Track habits.
  • Move projects on a kanban board.
  • Misc tasks go on sticky notes.
  • Think about how to start, at the start of each block.
  • Keep a scratchpad, ideally with a timer.
  • Pick the tasks to complete beforehand, ideally the day before.

Paper or app

WhereWhat
Sticky noteTomorrow’s picks, misc tasks, the day’s tally
Scratchpad with a timer (Antinote)The block’s timer, how to start, stray thoughts, the breakdown of each project into tasks, and other organizing
The appThe block log, habits, the project board, the week’s numbers

The start stays on paper. The app comes in only at the end, to log the finished block.

The app: Blocks

One page, four tabs. The screenshots show sample data.

Add. A yellow sticky note is the form: date, project, what I worked on, Add block. Under it: “Completed today, 4 / 6.” The target is a default for every day, and any day can have its own.

Habits. A week of circles, one row per habit, each with a goal in times per week. Green when the week’s goal is hit.

Projects. Three columns: To do, Started, Done. Each card shows blocks done out of a total, a week target and a due date. Drag a card to move it.

Reports. The week as a grid: blocks per project per day, green where a target is hit. For each started project, the rate I’m going at against the rate the due date needs: on track, or behind by so many blocks a week.

What it leaves out: no task list, no schedule, no timer, no reminders. Tasks are containers, and the app only counts what fills them: blocks against a project, habits against a week. The tasks themselves live on the sticky note, and the breakdown of a project into tasks lives in Antinote. The timer lives there too. It doesn’t keep what the work makes, either: notes from blocks and artifacts from projects go one level up, to Planka. Nothing in it waits for me to open it at the wrong moment.

The numbers

What I track: blocks, tasks and habits.

The numbers go in a database, and get reviewed weekly, or just in time, when a decision needs them. So the tool has to support that: every count is saved where I can look at it later, not only shown on today’s screen. Blocks saves every block and every habit tick, and the Reports tab is the weekly look.

Why this is enough

Each piece says one of the messages from the language of an ADHD system:

  • What now: the picks on the sticky note, made the night before (when this, do that).
  • How much: the count against the day’s target (a number on the task).
  • By when: the due date and the required rate on each project.
  • Done so far: the tally, and the log.
  • Not now: To do on the board. Parked, safe to forget.
  • Stop: the timer for a block, the target for the day.
  • You drifted: logging the block asks what it went to. If the answer isn’t the project, I know.

Take a piece away and one message goes quiet. Add a piece and it’s upkeep. This kit runs the focused work. Spruced Up Planka adds a layer on top: planning and resource management, deciding where the blocks go, so I only hold the work in progress in my attention. This is the version with nothing extra.
