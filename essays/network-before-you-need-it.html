Build the Network Before You Need It

November 2026. 397 words.

Keith Ferrazzi’s Never Eat Alone is about networking rather than negotiation. It covers what comes before any negotiation: building the relationships that give you options and allies. It’s Secure Your Position First, applied to people.

The core ideas

  • Don’t keep score. Make sure other people get what they want, too.
  • Ping constantly. Reach out to your contacts all the time, not just when you need something.
  • Never eat alone. Meals and other everyday hours are chances to meet people.

Underneath all of them is generosity. Ferrazzi writes that the focus should be on what you can do for people, not what they can do for you.

What the research says

Wide networks pay off. In 1973 Mark Granovetter argued for the strength of weak ties. In 2022 an experiment on LinkedIn tested it on over 20 million people across five years: moderately weak ties created the most job mobility. Casual acquaintances have networks that overlap less with yours, so they bring connections and information you couldn’t otherwise reach. That backs Ferrazzi’s habit of staying in touch with lots of people.

Generosity works, with a catch. Adam Grant found givers overrepresented at both the top and the bottom of success measures. The givers at the bottom get exploited. The ones at the top are what he calls otherish: a strong concern for others’ interests, and a strong degree of self-interest too.

How it connects to defense

  • Your network gives you alternatives. The strongest position in any negotiation is having a good alternative if the deal falls through. A network built before you need it gives you that.
  • Reputation compounds. Most of life is a repeated game, and there generosity builds on itself the way cooperation did in Axelrod’s tournament (Understand Power as Defense).
  • Otherish giving is the serpent and the dove together. Generous by default, with enough self-interest and boundaries not to be exploited (The Serpent and the Dove).

One warning: networking used purely as a tactic is just Cialdini’s reciprocity lever (The Art of Defense), and people can tell. It only works if the giving is real.

Before, then during

Networking is the before; negotiation is the during (Negotiation Needs Structure and Empathy). The network decides how strong you are when you sit down at the table. How you negotiate decides what the network thinks of you after.
