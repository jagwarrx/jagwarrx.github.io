Suspiciously Neat

November 2026. 455 words.

It’s not suspiciously neat. But here’s the case that it is.

The honest tension is that this fit may be suspiciously neat. The OSI analogy, my lens and the stack are all vertical models, and a conversation that starts with layers tends to find layers everywhere. Meadows’s actual world is built from loops that cut across levels: tiredness feeding poor sleep, reputation feeding opportunity. A life may be more like a web than a stack, and every model used here pushes toward seeing it as a stack. My lens warns about exactly this: a frame that can absorb everything should be audited before it’s trusted.

Legibility

The neatness isn’t an accident. It’s legibility: how easily a system can be read at a glance.

James C. Scott used the word for what states do to the things they manage. A forest becomes rows of one tree species, counted in board-feet. A village gets surnames, street names and a map. The messy original becomes something an official can read, count and steer.

A framework does the same for a life. A stack is legible: one thing on another, an order to check, a place for everything. A web of loops is truer and almost impossible to read.

Why the neatness isn’t the problem

A framework I can’t read, I won’t use. A system that needs a diagram of loops to work out what to do today gets abandoned by Wednesday. The ones that last are seen at a glance and read like a picture.

So the fit is neat because it was made to be neat. That’s a design choice, not a finding about the world. Being suspicious of it means asking a different question: not “is life really a stack?” but “what does reading it as a stack hide?”

What legibility costs

Scott’s other point: the legible version starts to replace the real one. The planted forest did well for a generation, then the soil and the missing species caught up with it. What the map couldn’t see, it couldn’t protect.

For a life, the hidden parts are the loops that cut across layers:

  • Tiredness and poor sleep feed each other.
  • Reputation brings opportunity, which builds reputation.
  • Every rep makes the next one easier, good or bad.
  • ADHD doesn’t sit in one layer. It touches all of them.

Keep both

  • Act on the legible version. The stack, day to day.
  • Review for the loops. In the weekly and quarterly review, ask which loop is running: what is feeding what? (Levels of Leverage)
  • Audit the frame. What can’t it express? (The Structural Lens)
  • Hold it lightly. It’s a map. Reality is too grand to be captured by one school of thought.
