The Language of Systems

November 2026. 527 words.

Every system has a language. Learning a system is mostly discovering the words and learning them.

Language is a tool. Humans are the tool-using animal, and language may be the tool that came with the others.

The words are the system

Each field has its own: stock, flow, loop and delay in systems thinking; backlog, WIP limit and done in kanban; category, constraint and default in the structural lens; state, log, queue and graph in the mechanics of a system.

Before I have the word, I can’t see the thing. The lens says it: no category, no question. After I have it, I see it everywhere. Most of the notes on this site are words I discovered that way: the wall of awful, alief, legibility, the monkey wrench.

Language is a tool

A hammer extends the hand. A word extends thought: it picks up a whole idea and lets me move it around. We shape our tools, and then our tools shape us. The words I have decide what I notice.

The link may run deep. Making a stone hand-axe and making a sentence both need a plan held in steps, and brain scans of people learning to knap stone show activity in regions that overlap with language. One idea in human evolution is that tools and language grew up together.

How language is stored and run

  • Not one spot. The old picture had two areas, Broca’s for speaking and Wernicke’s for understanding. The newer picture is a network, mostly on the left side: one stream links sounds to meaning, another links sounds to the mouth.
  • Meanings are spread out. Words are stored all over the cortex, near what they’re about. Words about people cluster together, as do words about places and numbers. Action words light up parts of the motor system.
  • Two kinds of memory. New words go into declarative memory, the kind I can look up and say out loud. Grammar ends up in procedural memory, the kind that runs without thinking, like riding a bike.

That last point is how learning any language feels. At first I look every word up. With use it becomes automatic, and I stop noticing I’m using it. Fluency is the move from looking up to just running. Every rep makes the next one easier here too.

Visual language

Words aren’t the only kind. Checkboxes, arrows, columns and boxes are a language read by the eyes, and for an ADHD brain they often work better than words. A kanban board is a sentence: this is waiting, this is in progress, this is done.

Learning the language of a system

  • Collect the words. Keep a glossary as I go. The concepts page is mine.
  • Use them in sentences. Explain something with the new words, from memory. Using them is the learning.
  • Notice a missing word. When I need a long sentence to point at something, it wants a name.
  • Coin one if none exists. A good name turns a vague feeling into a tool I can pick up.
  • Learn the visual words too. Every system has its diagrams. Learn to draw them, not just read them.
