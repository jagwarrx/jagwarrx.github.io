Concepts

Ideas I keep coming back to. Each one: what it is, where it comes from, and where it fits.

The Need

  • The Need: all humans, first and foremost, need to know what comes next
  • When they can’t, they reach for a story, a ritual or someone who claims to know
  • The Bundle: a religion sold, as one package, community, ritual, meaning, identity, ethics, a calendar, rites of passage, care for the sick and dying, a forecast and an answer to death
  • Unbundling: each part now sold separately, by gyms, wellness, podcasts, politics and astrology apps; the parts nobody buys alone are lost first
  • Rebundling: what gets unbundled gets bundled again, by the nation-state, wellness brands and political movements (Barksdale)

Programming

  • List Processing: all data structures can be represented as lists
  • Top Down Design
  • Bottom Up Design
  • Prolog: say what is true, then ask
  • The Relational Model: store each fact once
  • Types: make the wrong thing impossible to write
  • APL: work on the whole list at once

Systems

  • Open-loop system: follows a fixed plan and doesn’t check the result, like a toaster on a timer (control theory)
  • Closed-loop system: measures what’s happening and responds, like a thermostat; it doesn’t need a forecast (Know How You’ll Meet It)
  • Open and closed systems, the other sense: an open system trades energy and matter with its surroundings, like a cell or a firm; a closed one doesn’t (von Bertalanffy)

Business

  • Double Entry Bookkeeping: every amount comes from somewhere

Sociology

  • Performative Utterances: some words do something instead of describing it (Austin)
  • Institutional Facts: X counts as Y in context C (Searle)
  • Social Closure: a group raises its rewards by limiting who may enter (Weber)
  • Rent-Seeking: income from controlling access, not from making something (Tullock, Krueger)
  • Theodicy: an answer to why the innocent suffer (Weber)
  • Collective Effervescence: the charge a crowd feels in a shared rite (Durkheim)
  • Modes of Religiosity: frequent rites carry teaching, rare intense ones bind (Whitehouse)
  • Magic Under Uncertainty: magic for the open sea, none for the lagoon (Malinowski)
  • Cultural Evolution: ideas are copied with biases, and the biases decide what spreads (Boyd, Richerson, Henrich)
  • Minimally Counterintuitive Concepts: ordinary in every way but one, so easy to remember (Boyer)
  • Prestige Bias: copy those with status
  • Conformity Bias: copy what most people around you do
  • Credibility-Enhancing Displays: costly acts make a belief believable (Henrich)
  • Costly Signaling: the communes that demanded the most lasted longest (Sosis)
  • Multilevel Selection: selection works on groups and on individuals
  • Memes: sometimes the main beneficiary of an idea is the idea itself (Dennett)
  • Illusion of Control: when an outcome is uncertain, people act as if they can sway it (Langer)
  • Barnum Effect: a reading that fits everyone feels written for you (Forer)
  • Confirmation Bias: the hits are remembered, the misses forgotten
  • Falsifiability: a theory no observation could count against says nothing (Popper)
  • Protective Belt: blame each failure on an auxiliary hypothesis, never the core (Lakatos)
  • Duhem–Quine: every test checks a theory together with all its assumptions
  • Genetic Fallacy: judging a belief by where it came from

Psychology

  • Proximate and Ultimate Causes: how a behavior works now, and why it evolved (Mayr, Tinbergen)
  • Predictive Processing: the brain works to keep surprise low (Friston)
  • Psychological Entropy: uncertainty is felt as anxiety (Hirsh, Mar, Peterson)
  • The Interpreter: the brain makes up a confident story for what it didn’t cause (Gazzaniga)
  • Hyperactive Agency Detection: seeing agents and intent everywhere (Barrett, Guthrie)
  • Hazard-Precaution System: threats set off rigid, repeated, exact acts (Boyer, Liénard)
  • Error Management: evolution biases you toward the cheaper mistake (Haselton, Buss)
  • Smoke Detector Principle: alarms are set to go off too often (Nesse)
  • Superstitious Conditioning: random rewards breed rituals (Skinner)
  • Mental Time Travel: imagining the future, and so fearing it (Suddendorf, Corballis)
  • Need for Closure: seize the first answer that ends the doubt, then freeze on it (Kruglanski)
  • Compensatory Control: the less control people feel, the more order they believe in (Kay)
  • Evolutionary Mismatch: a trait tuned for one world, running in another
  • Byproduct or Adaptation: built for other jobs, or selected for itself (Boyer, Atran; Wilson)

ADHD

  • Time Blindness: only two times, now and not now (Barkley)
  • Delay Aversion: waiting hurts in itself (Sonuga-Barke)
  • Delay Discounting: a reward later counts for less than the same reward now
  • Default Mode Interference: the idle network fails to switch off during a task (Sonuga-Barke, Castellanos)
  • Exploration vs Exploitation: stay with the patch you know, or leave to look for a better one
