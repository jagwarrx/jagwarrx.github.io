Chess analyzer

Review your own games the way a coach would: say what you see first, then get questioned until you find the better move yourself.

Open full screen → · best on a laptop

How to use it

  • My games lists your recent Lichess games. Or paste a Lichess link or a PGN, pick your side, Load.
  • Step through with the arrow keys; W and E jump between your mistakes.
  • Socratic: write your read of the position first (plan, candidate, why). The coach then asks one or two questions at a time until you find it, and names the mistake type.
  • Coach review: the whole game in three lessons, credit, homework, a score for each phase, and one takeaway. From the second game on, it checks what you fixed and what keeps coming back.
  • 6-Factor and 1200/1700/2200 read a single position; Extract principle turns a chat into a rule you keep in Principles.
  • Turn the engine off (Engine: ON, top right) to analyse blind before you look.

What it knows, and what it doesn't

  • Claude can't see the board. Every prompt carries the position, the pieces left loose (attacked as often as defended), detected forks and the Lichess engine line, and Claude is told to use only those.
  • Engine data comes from Lichess. If a game has no computer analysis, request it on lichess.org; the app falls back to the Lichess cloud eval where it can.
  • Settings: your Claude API key, model, and your rating, which sets what the coach focuses on (one-move blunders under 1200, plans and structure higher up).

Your key, saved positions, principles and reviews stay in this browser; Settings exports them as a file. Game data goes to Claude only when you ask. A Socratic turn costs about 1 to 3 cents on Opus 5.5; a coach review about 10 to 20.
