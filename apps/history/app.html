About this chart

An original chart that keeps the side-by-side idea of printed world history wall charts and changes the parts that tend to mislead.

What is different

  • Uncertainty is drawn. A bar fades out where a date is traditional, disputed, or stands in for a gradual change. Tap a bar to see which.
  • States and cultures look different. Dashed bars are archaeological cultures, migrations or periods, which had no capital, ruler or border.
  • No Europe-based period names. The axis is plain dates. Labels like Classical Antiquity or Middle Ages fit Europe and little else.
  • Regions get equal standing. Sub-Saharan Africa, the Americas, the steppe, Southeast Asia and Oceania each have a full column.
  • Events are marked. Diamonds mark about 140 dated events, each in the region where it happened. Their names appear beside them when a column has room, so show one or two regions to read them.
  • Everything links out. Each bar, event and band has a Wikipedia link. Links go through Wikipedia's search, so a few may open a results page instead of an article.
  • One region can be regrouped. Select a single region and a Group by row appears. It re-sorts the same bars into columns by language, religion, way of life, trade network, ecology, polity type or claimed lineage.
  • Regions are linked. Hatched bands mark plagues, conquests, trade routes and climate shocks that crossed columns.
  • Two orientations. Vertical runs time down the page with regions side by side. Horizontal runs time left to right with one row per region.
  • Two time scales. An even scale is honest about duration. Stretch recent gives the crowded last five centuries three times the room.
  • The Roman Empire does not end in 476. The eastern empire is labelled as the Roman state continuing.

Limits

  • It is a selection of about 190 states and cultures and 140 events. Many states are missing, especially small or short-lived ones.
  • The Group by labels are one-word judgement calls about societies that were mixed and that changed. Each bar gets a single label per grouping, for its most typical or longest state. Treat them as a starting point.
  • Bar width means nothing. It does not show territory, population or importance.
  • Dates follow widely used conventional chronologies. Before about 1400 BCE, Near Eastern dates can shift by decades depending on the scheme.
  • Regions with fewer early bars had people and history too. Bars follow the surviving evidence, which favours societies that built in stone and wrote.
