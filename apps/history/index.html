World History in Eight Regions

5,300 years side by side, from Sumer to today. Eight regions with equal standing, and the uncertain dates drawn as fades instead of hidden.

Open full screen (best on a laptop)

How to use it

  • Search for a state, a person, an event or a year (like 1453 or 500 BCE). Press / to start typing.
  • Tap a bar, an event icon or an orange label for dates, a line of context, a picture from Wikipedia and a link to the article. The card also shows what came before and after, and what was going on elsewhere at the same time.
  • The red line marks one year. The map, in a small window or full size with the Map button, shows the borders in that year and where things happened, and The world in that year shows who was alive, what was changing and what comes next. Drag the slider, use the arrows or play to move it.
  • The buttons above the chart switch on events, shared events, links between regions and twelve overlays such as people, battles, faiths, trade and voices.
  • The region buttons above the chart show and hide regions. View picks Across or Down, even or stretched time, and how much detail.
  • Stories play like a short film across the chart and the map. Pause at any time to look around.
  • Show one region and Group by re-sorts its bars by language, religion, way of life, trade network, ecology, polity type or claimed lineage.
  • Share view copies a link to exactly what you see.

What it shows, and what it doesn't

  • A bar fades where its date is traditional, disputed or stands in for a gradual change. Dashed bars are cultures and movements, not states.
  • The axis is plain dates, with no European period names. Hatched bands mark plagues, conquests, trade routes and climate shocks that crossed regions.
  • It is a selection: about 190 states and cultures and over 300 events and battles. Bar width means nothing, and the bars favour societies that built in stone and wrote.
  • The 5,900 short pages behind the chart were written with Claude from general knowledge. They are not fact-checked. Use them as a way in, and check before you rely on one.
