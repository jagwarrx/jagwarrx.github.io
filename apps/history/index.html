World History in Eight Regions

5,300 years side by side, from Sumer to today. Eight regions with equal standing, and the uncertain dates drawn as fades instead of hidden.

Open full screen (best on a laptop)

How to use it

  • Horizontal (the default) runs time left to right with one row per region. Vertical runs time down the page with the regions side by side. Your choice is remembered.
  • Tap a bar, an event icon or an orange label for dates, a line of context, a picture from Wikipedia and a link to the article.
  • Each event has a small icon for its kind: crossed swords for wars and battles, a ship for voyages, a book for writing, a crown for rulers.
  • Zoom sets how many events show. Zoomed out, only the major ones. Zoom in and notable events, then details, join them; the major ones get bigger.
  • Tap one region to show only it. A Group by row appears that re-sorts its bars by language, religion, way of life, trade network, ecology, polity type or claimed lineage.
  • Stretch recent gives the crowded last five centuries three times the room. Zoom with the minus and plus buttons.

What it shows, and what it doesn't

  • A bar fades where its date is traditional, disputed or stands in for a gradual change. Dashed bars are cultures and movements, not states.
  • The axis is plain dates, with no European period names. Hatched bands mark plagues, conquests, trade routes and climate shocks that crossed regions.
  • It is a selection: about 190 states and cultures and 140 events. Bar width means nothing, and the bars favour societies that built in stone and wrote.
