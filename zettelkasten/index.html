  • The Other: Trust the System, Not the Brain · Nov 2026Why an ADHD brain needs something outside itself: a system, a person, a place. The inner voice that says not now, do this is weak, so the other has to come from outside. In the moment, the system outranks the brain.
  • The Language ADHD Speaks · Nov 2026ADHD processes a certain kind of language: now, seen, felt, concrete and short. It barely hears importance, the future, abstractions or should. So managing ADHD is mostly translation: the plan is written in importance, and the day has to run in now.
  • The Language of Systems · Nov 2026Every system has a language, and learning a system is mostly discovering its words and learning them. Language is a tool, maybe the first one: in the brain, making tools and making sentences share ground. Once I have the word, I can see the thing.
  • Is Behavior the Language of Life? · Nov 2026Behavior communicates something. Words, behaviors and deeds are all saying something about you, and deeds say it loudest. Gandhi called his life a set of experiments with truth. This is probably what is meant by character.
  • Modelling ADHD Attention as a Stack · Nov 2026ADHD attention works like a stack: the last thing in is the first thing out, and only the top is top of mind. Importance doesn’t move an item up; only being new does. So build the priority queue outside, and let the head be a stack.
  • Suspiciously Neat · Nov 2026The stack, the lens and the OSI model are all vertical, and life may be more like a web. But the neatness isn’t a mistake: it’s legibility, the thing that makes a framework usable. Use the neat frame to act, and look for the loops when you review.
  • The Structural Lens and the Stack · Nov 2026The stack of effectiveness is the anatomy of a life: what the parts are and which serves which. The structural lens is the physiology of a choice: how a decision moves from frame to outcome and back. Each shows what the other can't see.
  • Improvised Productivity · Nov 2026Start a timer, then figure it out. Novelty plays a key role, and a timer, money and people each add a sense of stakes. Three ways it fails, four small fixes, and one honest test.
  • The ADHD Monkey Wrench: Diffused Attention · Nov 2026Diffused attention affects sustained attention. I can focus only when something is novel or high stakes, so important things wait until they seem dangerous and new things get done at once. The fix is to borrow the stakes early, on purpose.
  • Levels of Leverage: Where to Intervene · Nov 2026Donella Meadows ranked twelve places to intervene in a system. Mapped onto the stack of effectiveness: direction holds the most leverage, plans the least, and the loops between layers are where most fixes live. Diagnose bottom-up, intervene top-down.
  • Every Rep Makes the Next One Easier · Nov 2026Superstitions are self-perpetuating, so it’s better to nip them in the bud. Same with bad habits, mental and behavioral: every rep makes the next one easier. Knowing a belief is wrong doesn’t change the behavior; new experience does.
  • Direction, Time, Energy · Nov 2026Seven layers, from what it’s all for down to the fuel. Direction at the top, time in the middle, energy at the bottom. If any one of them is zero, the whole result is zero.
  • Active Learning Is the Only Learning · Nov 2026Reading is not learning. Reading is familiarizing. Thinking, reasoning and recalling are learning.
  • Learning with the Socratic Method · Nov 2026The best use of an LLM: not answers, but a tutor that asks one question at a time, aimed at the joints of my reasoning, so the shallow spots surface and get fixed.
  • Log the Block, Don’t Start It · Nov 2026One button to log, with two types: blocks and habits. The block starts on a sticky note; the app only logs it once it’s done.
  • Layers of Operations · Nov 2026Plan, capture, choose, work, log. A personal system designed like a software system: requirements, entities, flow, layers, then the tools. A tool can be swapped; a layer can’t be skipped.
  • Three Layers of Dysregulation · Nov 2026Jenna Free’s frame: a lot of my ADHD struggle is a nervous system stuck in fight-or-flight. Work on it in three layers, in order: calm the body, check the thoughts, then change the behavior.
  • The Block Method · Nov 2026David Cain’s method for the productivity-challenged: 25 minutes, uninterrupted, aimed at finishing a task, tallied on a sticky note. Any goal is a number of Blocks. And it climbs the Wall of Awful, because a Block never asks for the whole task, only for the next 25 minutes.
  • Choosing a Task · Nov 2026The gap between the best task and the third-best is usually smaller than the time spent agonizing over it. Choose fast: before I need to, with one fixed tiebreaker, from a menu of three, in sixty seconds, two at a time.
  • The Wall of Awful · Nov 2026Brendan Mahan’s name for the emotional pile-up behind a task: every bad attempt and every bit of guilt adds a brick, until the hard part is the feelings, not the task. Four ways to meet it, how to climb it in small kind steps, and how to keep it low.
  • Habits That Sustain · Nov 2026The pyramid of habits. At the base: exercise, water, medication. On the next level: hygiene, nutrition, sleep, planning. Each level holds up the one above. On a bad day the base is the whole goal, and when the week falls apart, repair from the bottom.
  • If You Don’t See It, It Doesn’t Exist · Nov 2026If I don’t see it, it doesn’t exist: tasks buried in a project, a deadline next week, a tool I don’t open, options spread across places. It’s also a lever. Put in view what should stay alive, hide what should go away, and decide what’s in view on purpose.
  • See Everything Together in One Place · Nov 2026To make a decision, I need to see everything together on one master board. Out of sight is out of existence, so with things spread out, every choice comes with doubt about what I’m missing. One board closes the set. Decide on the board, work from the short list.
  • The Unusual Effectiveness of Visual Language for ADHD · Nov 2026Bullet journals, bullets, checkboxes, mind maps, kanban boards and canvases. For an ADHD brain, marks and shapes do organizing work that words do badly, and short words beat sentences. So build a language for organization: a few signs, one meaning each, the same everywhere.
  • When It’s Not Working, Start · Nov 2026Usually, when something is not working, I just need to start. Most failures I notice are things that never ran. Make the start tiny, name the first physical action, and start before I feel ready. Start first, fix second.
  • Always Work Backwards in a Plan · Nov 2026Start at the finish line and walk back to something I can do today. The walk also shows what kind of problem I have: where it goes through cleanly, I don’t need more strategy, just raw firepower. Only where it stalls do I need to think.
  • The Unreasonable Effectiveness of a Number · Nov 2026Review 10 cards. Five blocks on the essay. A number on a task, as long as it isn’t time, gives it an end, a size and a score I can watch go up. It works far better than it should.
  • When This, Do That · Nov 2026The art of planning ahead is moving every decision out of the moment I act and into a calm moment before it, ideally the night before. When this happens, I do that. Then the only question left is whether to start.
  • Think in Trees, Do in Lists · Nov 2026My ADHD brain thinks like a mind map, in trees, not in a line like a list. That’s good for thinking and bad for doing. So I split them: think in mind maps, then force the doing into a list, one item after another.
  • Compounding in the Real World · Nov 2026On paper compounding is a smooth curve. In a life it’s messy and mostly invisible: it comes late, in lumps, after a lag, through other people, into limits and sometimes in reverse, with no statement to show the stock. Track the inputs, judge it in years, and don’t interrupt it.
